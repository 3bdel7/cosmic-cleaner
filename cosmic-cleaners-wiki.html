The Loop

01

Fly

Launch from your base with a jetpack. Speed and thrust set how fast you cover ground.

02

Collect

Your equipped magnet pulls scrap in automatically. Range and pull decide how wide and how fast.

03

Fill up

Scrap fills your storage. Capacity comes from the jetpack — 60 slots on the starter Scout MK-I, up to 210 on the Astra Mk.V.

04

Process

Crushers and furnaces on your base plot turn scrap into ingots. The plot starts at 2×2 and expands to 5×5.

05

Sell

Sell ingots through the Remote Market Uplink. The market swing and your boosts set the payout.

Currencies

Credits

Earned by selling ingots. Buys every jetpack, magnet, furnace and crusher in the game. The whole calculator on this wiki exists to predict this number.

Robux

Roblox premium currency. Every purchasable item has a Robux price alongside its credit price, letting you skip the grind. Gamepasses (2X Credits, VIP Crew) are Robux-only.

Magnets

15 catalogued · 5 tiers

Bought at the Magnet Emporium, sorted into five material tiers. Magnets work automatically once equipped — range is the pickup radius in studs, pull is the strength multiplier. Base & Plot

2×2 → 5×5

Your base is a domed planet holding the machine plot, the market uplink and the shops. The plot is where every furnace and crusher sits, and its size is the hard ceiling on how much scrap you can process.

The Plot

STARTING SIZE2 × 2 — 4 cells
MAXIMUM SIZE5 × 5 — 25 cells
V1 / V2 FOOTPRINT¼ cell — 4 per cell
V3 / V4 / V5 FOOTPRINT1 full cell
MAX FURNACE THROUGHPUT25 scrap/s
EXPANSIONbought cell by cell, credits

A full plot of Solar V2 furnaces means 100 units. The same output from Supernova V5 needs only 25 units, but costs twice as much.

Furnaces and crushers sit on separate plots. They do not compete for cells, so each machine type’s throughput ceiling stands on its own.

Machine Handling

CRUSH MATERIALF key, at the crusher
COLLECT INGOTSE key, at the furnace

Machines buffer their output rather than delivering it automatically — a furnace was observed holding 1,073 ingots awaiting collection, and no capacity ceiling has been found. The Piping System unlock feeds crushed scrap into furnaces automatically, but ingot collection still appears to be manual.

Typical Player Behaviour By Stage

Observed patterns in how players actually sell, which matters far more than optimal play when modelling pacing.

StageTypical sell pointBoosts in useEffective ingot value
Rebirth 1–3~+15% marketNo event awareness, no crew, no gamepasses~11.5 cr

  • New players do not know events exist. For the first two or three rebirths they sell whenever their inventory fills, landing near the middle of the market range rather than waiting for a peak.
  • Event timing is the single biggest earning skill and it is entirely undiscovered early on. A player who learns to sell during Ingot Rush earns roughly 60% more per ingot than one who does not, with no purchases involved.
  • This gap means early pacing must be modelled at roughly +15% market with no multipliers, not at optimal play. Any tuning based on optimal play will make the opening far slower than intended.

Gather Rate — measured

A full round trip — leave base, fill storage, return, unload — takes 1 to 2 minutes, averaging about 90 seconds. Against 435 storage that fixes the effective gathering rate:

Footprint Changes Everything — V5 Is A Trap

Furnaces do not all occupy the same space. V1 and V2 take a quarter of a plot cell each, so four fit per cell. V3, V4 and V5 each take a full cell. That single fact inverts the entire tier ladder.

  • The maximum possible furnace output is 25 scrap/s on a full 5×5 plot, reachable with either Solar V2 or Supernova V5 — but Solar costs 30,000 credits against Supernova’s 60,000.
  • Three of five tiers are dead content. V3, V4 and V5 are each strictly beaten by a cheaper tier at equal or lower cost. A player who saves for the top furnace is worse off than one who bought the second-cheapest.
  • The speed values themselves are clean. V5 really is five times V1 per unit, and the crusher multipliers mirror the furnace seconds exactly. The imbalance comes entirely from pricing and footprint, not from the speed curve.

Crushers Are Built Correctly

Crushers occupy their own plot, separate from furnaces. Every crusher tier takes a full plot cell, with no quarter-cell exception. That keeps the ladder honest: more expensive always means more throughput per cell, and the top tier is genuinely the best.

With space limited, buy the highest crusher tier you can afford. Titan delivers five times a Scrap Crusher in the same footprint. Cheaper tiers cost less per unit of speed, but that only matters if you have spare cells — and you never do.

Design contrast Crushers and furnaces share the same speed curve and were written in the same file, yet crushers reward upgrading and furnaces punish it. The difference is entirely the quarter-cell footprint on furnace V1 and V2. That strongly suggests the furnace inversion is an oversight rather than a deliberate choice.

  • Base crush time per scrap unknown, so crusher output cannot be expressed in absolute terms.
  • Star upgrade interaction unmeasured. Furnace Turbocharge and Crusher Overdrive each add +15% speed per level over five levels. Whether that is additive, multiplicative, or applied to the raw time value is untested.
  • Plot expansion cost unknown. Players begin with a 2×2 plot and build toward 5×5, but the cost per expansion is not recorded.
  • Machine capacity appears unlimited. No cap has been observed on how many ingots a furnace or how much material a crusher will hold. If a ceiling exists it has not been reached.
  • Scrap storage cost varies by type — needs mapping. Different scrap occupies different amounts of storage, and each yields at least as many ingots as the space it consumes. Types seen in the hotbar include Meteor, Asteroid, Broken variants, Dirty variants. The per-type storage cost and ingot yield are unrecorded, and this affects every credits-per-hour figure.
  • Plot expansion cost per cell unknown. Cells are bought individually with credits from 2×2 up to 5×5.
  • Shop stock limits purchases. The Shopkeeper’s Storage frequently reads “None”, so filling a plot with 100 Solar furnaces may be gated by restock timing rather than credits.

Market & Ingots

Verified

Ingots are sold through the Remote Market Uplink. The market swing changes on a timer of roughly two to three minutes and applies to base ingot value before any boost.

  • Normal ingot base = 10 credits. Sell price = 10 × (1 + market%). Matches the in-game panel exactly at every swing observed.
  • Charged ingot base = 30 credits — exactly 3× normal. Back-solved from a paired sale: the same 488-ingot cargo at −18% moved from 11,762 to 14,076 when 32 charged ingots were added.
  • Swing is capped at ±30%. Every one of the nine verified sales falls inside it — −28, −18, −15, −11, +13, +15, +25. Any market reading beyond ±30% means an event is folded into the number.
  • Update timer: 3 minutes. A countdown on the market panel shows time to the next roll.
  • Readings above the cap are event-inflated. The +86% and +80% figures recorded were both during events. This is independent confirmation that some events add to the market number rather than multiplying separately — see Events.
  • Display rounding: the game rounds swing % to whole numbers and price to one decimal, so true values sit fractionally below what's shown. This is the entire source of the ≤0.20% error in the calculator — not a hidden fee.
  • Two separate sales both displayed −11% / 8.9 but back-solve to slightly different underlying prices. Different rolls that rounded to the same display. Expected, not a contradiction.

Boosts & Passes

4 known

Every boost is a multiplier applied to the market-adjusted sale value, and they compound with each other. Additive math is measurably wrong — see Sale Log.

  • Golden Touch — upgrade, +10% credits per level, max level 5 (×1.50 at cap). Verified at level 4 (×1.40) across all eight logged sales.
  • 2X Credits Pass — gamepass, ×2.00 exactly. Verified in every logged sale.
  • Crew boost — +5% per friend online. Verified at one friend (×1.05). Cap unknown.
  • VIP Crew Pass — gamepass. In-game text: +10% credits on sales, a VIP name tag, and a free daily crate. Untested Multiplier not owned or measured — the ×1.10 in the calculator comes from the tooltip alone.

Events

2 catalogued

Timed server-wide events that boost sale value. Only one runs at a time. This section is actively incomplete — more events exist and are still being found.

Star Flare×1.70–×1.90 · 40 min · verified

Runs for 40 minutes, and the bonus is not fixed — it drifts between +70% and +90%, rerolling every few minutes. It applies as an independent multiplier on top of the market price, and does not appear in the market percentage.

The logged sale caught it at +75%, and fits ×1.75 to within 0.01% — the tightest match in the entire log:

base 5,840  (488 normal + 32 charged)
× 0.85 market (−15%)  → 4,964
× 1.40 Golden Touch 4  → 6,949.60
× 2.00 2X Credits  → 13,899.20
× 1.75 Star Flare  → 24,323.60
actual payout  → 24,322  (−0.01%)

Confirmed Crew was offline for this sale, confirmed by the player. That rules out the alternative reading (crew active, event ×1.667) and establishes that Star Flare multiplies independently, exactly like every other boost.

Because the bonus drifts, timing matters. Selling at +90% instead of +70% is roughly 12% more credits on the same cargo. Over a 40-minute window that is worth watching the banner before committing a large sale.

Ingot Rush+75 to market swing · confirmed

Banner appears top-left with a depleting bar. Countdowns of 2:33 and 1:46 observed.

Confirmed Ingot Rush adds its 75% to the market swing rather than multiplying on top of it, so the boost is already inside the market percentage the game displays. Two independent lines of evidence:

1. It breaks the cap. Market swing is capped at ±30%. The +86% seen during a rush is impossible as a base roll — it can only be +11% base with +75 from the event folded in.

2. A rush sale fits once the event is counted once. 256 ingots at the displayed +86% (18.6/ingot) with Golden Touch 4 and 2X, no crew, gives 256 × 18.6 × 2.80 = 13,332.48 against an actual 13,330 — a 0.02% deviation, identical to every other clean sample. That sale only ever looked broken because the event was being applied twice.

One sample still unexplained A second rush sale (80 ingots at +86% → 5,600, implying 70.00/ingot) fits no combination of known boosts. Boost state was not logged. Left excluded.

  • Events do not share one mechanic. Star Flare multiplies on top of the displayed market price. Ingot Rush folds into the market swing, so the panel number already contains it. Always check which type is running before trusting a projection — getting it wrong changes the payout by tens of thousands of credits on a large cargo.
  • A market reading beyond ±30% is the tell. Base swing cannot exceed ±30%, so anything higher means an event is baked into the number and must not be applied again.
  • Both known events headline at 75%, but Star Flare drifts between 70% and 90% across its runtime while Ingot Rush has only been seen at a flat 75.
  • No event has been observed running simultaneously with another.

Rebirth Economy

Formula verified

Rebirth resets progress in exchange for a Star. The cost follows an exact exponential: each rebirth costs 40% more than the one before it. Since gathering rate is capped by inventory and finite upgrades, the gap between cost and income widens permanently.

The Formula

cost = 2,500 × 1.4n

Where n is your current rebirth count. First rebirth costs 2,500. Verified against rebirth 15 → 16: 2,500 × 1.415 = 388,919.94, displayed in game as 388,920.

Why It Walls

  • Cost grows exponentially, income does not. Every rebirth is 40% dearer than the last, but gathering rate is bounded by inventory capacity and a finite upgrade tree. Once upgrades are exhausted the two curves diverge and never reconverge.
  • Rebirth 15 → 16 costs 388,920 — roughly 40% of the 966,051 credits that all fifteen prior rebirths cost put together. The next single step is nearly half the entire game to date.
  • The Offline Fleet does not close the gap. Autonomous Fleet produces 1,920 ingots per 6-hour cycle. Rebirth 15 → 16 needs roughly 11,785 ingots at a neutral market — about 37 hours of uninterrupted drone output, with a claim required every 6 hours.
  • Stars accelerate, cost accelerates faster. Star income steps up at rebirth 5 and again at rebirth 10, reaching 3 per rebirth. But cost multiplies by 1.4 every single step, so the star curve is linear against an exponential.
  • Market timing helps but cannot rescue it. Selling at the +30% ceiling instead of a neutral market saves about 23% of the required ingots. Against a cost curve multiplying by 1.4 each step, that is absorbed within one rebirth.

Star Exchange

32 nodes mapped · income rule unconfirmed

Stars are the prestige currency, earned by rebirthing and spent in the Star Exchange on permanent upgrades and unlocks that persist through every reset. The tree has 24 upgrade levels across 6 upgrades, plus 8 one-time unlocks — 32 nodes total.

Star Income

Despite the interface reading “+1 star per rebirth”, the payout scales with how many rebirths you have:

REBIRTHS 1–41 star each
REBIRTHS 5–92 stars each
REBIRTHS 10+3 stars each

This rule reproduces the 32 stars earned shown on a rebirth-15 account exactly: (4×1) + (5×2) + (6×3) = 32. Running totals would be 4 at rebirth 4, 14 at rebirth 9, 32 at rebirth 15, 47 at rebirth 20, 77 at rebirth 30.

Not confirmed. Stars can also be bought with Robux in the Cosmic Store, and that account had purchased some. If purchased stars count toward the “Stars Earned” total, the match above is coincidence rather than evidence. Confirming this needs an account that has never bought stars, or a known purchase total to subtract.

  • Star Vault compounds. Keeping 25% of credits through each reset effectively cuts the next rebirth requirement by a quarter, and that saving repeats every single rebirth thereafter.
  • Drone Uplink is the online-drone feature. The Offline Fleet only runs while you are away unless this is unlocked.
  • Deep Pockets adds on top of jetpack storage. Up to 15 slots across its three levels. Now a much smaller share of the total than before the jetpack rebalance.
  • Whether star costs are flat or scale per level is open. The prices listed are what the interface showed for the next purchase. Charging those rates flat for every owned node on a rebirth-15 account totals 32★ of upgrades plus a share of unlocks — roughly 38★ spent — against 32 earned and 9 remaining. That 15★ gap is close to the size of a Robux star purchase on the same account, so flat pricing may well be correct. It cannot be settled without knowing the exact purchase total.
  • Scrap Fortune’s “+1 luck” is unquantified. No measured effect on rare scrap rates.
  • Star Crate contents unknown. No lootcrate roll has been recorded.

Feedback Log

For developer report

Findings and suggestions gathered for the balance and pacing report. Kept here so nothing is lost between sessions.

Balance findingsprovable from game values
  • Furnace tiers V3, V4 and V5 are strictly dominated. Because V1 and V2 take a quarter cell while V3 upward take a full cell, a full plot of Solar V2 matches Supernova V5 output exactly at half the cost (30,000 vs 60,000 credits). Plasma V3 is beaten by Ember V1 at identical cost. The most expensive furnace in the game is the worst purchase.
  • Crusher and furnace speeds and prices both need rebalancing, independently of the footprint issue.
  • Crushers do not have this problem — every tier takes a full cell, so the ladder rewards upgrading properly. The same speed curve produces opposite outcomes purely because of footprint, which points to the furnace case being an oversight.
  • Vortex X is a dead purchase. At 65.7 credits per storage slot it is the worst step in the jetpack line. Buying Nova Pulse then going straight to Astra saves 3,500 credits for the same end state.
  • Rebirth cost grows exponentially against bounded income. Cost is 2,500 × 1.4n while gathering rate is capped by storage and a finite upgrade tree. Rebirth 15 alone costs roughly 40% of every prior rebirth combined.
  • Storage may outrun processing. 435 storage against a 25 scrap/s furnace ceiling means a full load is consumed in about 17 seconds. If a round trip takes longer than that, machines idle and additional furnaces buy nothing. Needs round-trip timing to confirm.
Interface suggestionscheap, high value
  • Replace the “Crushing…” and “Smelting…” labels with the amount of scrap inside the machine. The current text says only that work is happening, which the player can already see from the animation. Showing the contents tells them how full the machine is, how long until it finishes, and whether it is worth returning yet — turning a decorative label into a decision-making tool.
  • Show total pending ingots across all furnaces rather than requiring the player to walk to each one to discover a backlog.
Pacing & progression notesraw — for the pacing report

Design intent captured during discussion. Not yet planned or costed.

  • Target shape: a sawtooth. Time per rebirth climbs, then an affordable upgrade cuts it back, then climbs again past the previous peak. Specifically rebirth 6 should take about as long as rebirth 4.
  • Early push. The opening rebirths should come quickly to hook the player before the curve steepens.
  • Furnace Vault is the expected first star purchase, because ingots accumulate inside furnaces and survive rebirth if the machines do.
  • Scout MK-I starting storage of 60 is far too high. The opening backpack should be much smaller so that storage upgrades have somewhere to grow from and early trips create a real reason to return to base. Two options to weigh: add more jetpack tiers to lengthen the ladder, or rescale storage across the existing five. Undecided which.
  • Furnaces and crushers should have a maximum capacity. At present a furnace was observed holding over a thousand ingots with no ceiling, which removes any pressure to return and collect. A cap turns collection into a real decision and gives machine tiers a second axis to differentiate on. Cap values undecided.
  • Zone differentiation plan. Zones are currently near-identical. Proposed direction: zone 2 seeds 10–15% of its scrap as charged, with zones 3, 4 and 5 escalating from there, so travelling further carries a real reward. Zones would then be the multiplier that lets a steep rebirth curve survive.
  • Scrap storage weight needs mapping. Different scrap types take different amounts of storage but always yield at least as many ingots as the space they occupy. Flagged to revisit — it changes every throughput figure.
  • Plot should start at 2×2 and expand cell by cell with credits to 5×5, as it currently does. Confirmed as intended behaviour rather than something to change.
  • Furnace overcapacity. At the measured gather rate of 4.83 units/sec, five cells of Solar V2 absorb everything the player can carry. A full 25-cell plot runs at 19% utilisation. Either gathering must scale far higher, or the plot is several times larger than the loop can feed.
  • Magnets and jetpacks should be gated behind rebirth count, not price alone, and repriced relative to the rebirth cost at the point they become available.
  • 15 magnets total to distribute across the rebirth ladder. Pricing should scale with how hard the intended acquisition point is.
  • Gear never resets once bought (Gear Locker), so an early purchase is permanent power. Scaling must prevent a player skipping ahead to a far-tier magnet before the matching storage exists to use it.
  • Crusher and furnace speeds and prices both need changing as part of the rebalance, not just the footprint issue.

  • With the gear that exists today, the cost exponent has to be around ×1.20 for a 30-rebirth game to stay inside a sane time budget.
  • Keeping ×1.40 requires roughly 1,900× gather-rate growth. Storage and magnets cannot supply that. Only zones with multiplied scrap density can, which makes zones a mathematical requirement rather than a content preference.
  • The fork for the report: lower the exponent to about 1.20 and tune with gear, or keep a steep exponent and commit to zones as the primary progression system. Neither can be skipped.
Structural suggestionsfrom earlier report
  • Gate zones behind rebirth count, each meaningfully different, so rebirth becomes a destination rather than a counter.
  • Automation should be a floor, not a ceiling. Drone Uplink exists but is buried behind a 3-star unlock; offline output caps at 6 hours and holds until claimed.
  • Gate progression by rebirth count, not star cost, since stars are purchasable and rebirths are not.
  • Give charged ingots a role beyond being worth more credits.
  • Contracts, team missions, risk zones and event variety as content sources that scale without hand-building.
  • The concept is unimplemented, not missing. NASO sending workers on missions is the stated premise, but there are no missions, no rank, and no company relationship in the game.

Why multiplicative, not additive

At Golden Touch 4 + 2X + 1 crew, adding the bonuses gives +145% → ×2.45. Compounding them gives 1.40 × 2.00 × 1.05 = ×2.94. On the 488-ingot sale at +25%, additive predicts 14,945 credits. The actual payout was 17,934 — exactly what compounding predicts.
