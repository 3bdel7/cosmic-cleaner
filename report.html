Part One

The Game Today, And What Breaks It

The core loop works and the recent storage patch was a real improvement. What follows is not about difficulty — it is four places where the game's own numbers work against it, each provable from measured play or from the game's source values.

Verified sale formula

total = [ (10 × normal) + (30 × charged) ]
  × (1 + market%) × (1 + event%)
  × (1 + GT lv × 10%)
  × (VIP ? 1.10 : 1) × (2X ? 2.00 : 1)
  × (1 + crew × 5%)

Verified against ten real sales, maximum error 0.20%, all of it explained by the game rounding market percentages for display. Bonuses compound — they do not add.

01
Dominated options · almost certainly a bug

The most expensive furnace in the game is the worst purchase in the game

From the game's own source, furnace smelt times run 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 seconds per scrap across V1–V5 — a clean fivefold gain. But V1 and V2 occupy a quarter of a plot cell each while V3 upward occupy a full cell. Once footprint is counted, the ladder inverts.

A player who saves for the top furnace ends up strictly worse off than one who bought the second-cheapest. Crushers do not have this problem — every crusher tier takes a full cell, so their ladder rewards upgrading correctly. Same speed curve, same source file, opposite outcome. That contrast is the strongest evidence this is unintended rather than designed.

02
Economy · caps the game's lifespan

Exponential cost against bounded income

Rebirth cost is exactly 2,500 × 1.4n, verified to the credit. Every rebirth costs 40% more than the last, forever, while gathering rate is bounded by storage, magnet pull and a finite upgrade tree. Two curves that diverge never reconverge.

RebirthCostTime for that single rebirth
10 → 1172,3144 minutes
15 → 16388,92022 minutes
20 → 212,091,7062 hours
25 → 2611,249,69910.6 hours
30 → 3160,503,58157 hours

Rebirth 15 alone costs roughly 40% of what all fourteen prior rebirths cost combined. The storage patch moved the wall from rebirth 15 to about rebirth 20 — worth doing, but the shape is unchanged.

Holding a thirty-rebirth game inside a sane time budget needs roughly nine- to fifteen-fold time growth. The current gear ladder offers about 26× of gather-rate headroom in total. At ×1.4, cost grows 17,287× across thirty rebirths and would need 1,921× rate growth to stay playable. Storage and magnets cannot supply that. Only multiplied scrap density can — which is why zones are not a content preference but a mathematical requirement.

03
Systems mismatch · removes a whole progression track

The base processes five times more than a player can deliver

A round trip averages 90 seconds and returns 435 units, fixing the effective gathering rate at 4.83 units per second. A full 5×5 plot of Solar V2 furnaces processes 25 units per second.

Furnace capacity, full plot25.0 units/sec
Actual demand from gathering4.83 units/sec
Utilisation19%
Cells actually needed5
Cells doing nothing20

A full load is consumed in 17 seconds, then the furnaces idle for the remaining 73 seconds of every trip. Expanding the plot past roughly five usable cells buys nothing — which quietly removes plot expansion as a goal and makes most of the furnace shop irrelevant.

04
Content · the reason players stop

There is no content source outside the grind

Zones are almost identical, so travelling further is never rewarded and the map beyond the nearest belt is decoration. But the deeper issue is what that reveals: the game's only content source is the number curve. When the numbers stop being interesting, content stops entirely.

Every game that retains on this platform generates content from somewhere the developers are not hand-building each week — other players (trading, social, co-op), randomness (rare drops, mutations, weather), or skill and builds (loadouts, minmaxing, PvP). Cosmic Cleaners currently has none of the three.

There is also a stated concept that never reached the game. The premise is that NASO dispatches workers on missions to clear scrap, and that workers upgrade their bases. There are no missions, no rank, no company relationship, and the base is a sell point rather than a project. The concept is not missing — it is unimplemented. That is a far easier problem to fix, and Part 3 builds directly on it.

Part Two

Every Recommendation

Everything gathered from play, from source values, and from community discussion — sorted by how much work each takes against how much it returns. Part 3 then applies them.

Quick wins · ship this week

  • Fix the furnace footprint inversion. Normalise footprints or reprice V3–V5. Three of five tiers are dead content today.
  • Show scrap counts inside machines instead of "Crushing…" and "Smelting…". The animation already says work is happening; the number says how full and whether to come back.
  • Show total pending ingots across all furnaces, so a backlog is visible without walking to each one.
  • Put a countdown on every event, with a hover tooltip explaining what it does.
  • Add an index or codex for scrap, jetpacks, magnets, furnaces, crushers. Players are reverse-engineering this from screenshots.
  • Show effectiveness when buying a crusher or furnace — a plain "processes X per second" line.
  • Let players customise portal colour. Portals are currently easy to confuse.
  • Give every furnace and crusher a capacity ceiling. One was observed holding 1,073 ingots with no cap, which removes any pressure to return.

Balance · needs a tuning pass

  • Moderate the rebirth cost curve. ×1.4 forever cannot work against bounded income.
  • Rescale jetpack storage. A 60-slot starter leaves the ladder nowhere to grow and makes the first upgrade invisible.
  • Reprice Vortex X or cut it. At its live numbers it is the worst cost-per-slot in the line — a trap purchase.
  • Rebalance crusher and furnace speed and price together once footprint is fixed.
  • Gate gear behind rebirth count, not price. Stars are purchasable; rebirth count is not. Only rebirth gates survive contact with the store.
  • Move respawn to the hub and shuffle portal order. Spawning at base enables a rejoin macro — dump scrap, fly out, rejoin to teleport home.
  • Let the remote market sell scrap as well as ingots, or its Robux price is hard to justify.
  • Keep automation strictly below active play, permanently. Drones should be the floor for players who cannot grind, never the optimal strategy.
  • Raise or remove the 6-hour offline drone cap and tie it to upgrades — it currently works as a login tether rather than automation.

Structural · the actual retention work

  • Differentiate zones and gate them behind rebirth. Highest-impact change available, and the only thing that makes a steep curve survivable.
  • Implement the stated concept. NASO contracts, company rank, sector assignments — the fantasy already exists in item text.
  • Give charged ingots a role beyond being worth more: a second currency for zone unlocks and high-tier gear.
  • Contracts and bounties. Rotating timed objectives give a goal for the next fifteen minutes instead of an open-ended grind.
  • Team missions and co-op zones. Group jobs that pay more turn other players into content — the only content source that scales without dev time.
  • Event variety. Both known events are +75% to sale value. Vary the type, not the size.
  • Risk zones as a mini-game. High density, real danger, cargo lost on death. Rewards skill instead of time.
  • Give merchants a character. Rivet is a vending machine. A face, a line of dialogue, a daily quest.
  • Milestone cosmetics by rebirth tier. Visible progress is its own retention loop.
  • Something beyond rebirth 30. Seasons, mastery, leaderboards — the curve should not be the only endgame.

The principle underneath all of it

Gate by rebirth count, never by price alone. Stars can be bought with Robux; rebirth count cannot. Any gate keyed to a star or credit price can be skipped with money, and any gate keyed to rebirth number cannot.

This protects the economy without touching monetisation, and it is a better product for paying players too — buying stars still speeds them toward gates, it simply cannot carry them past one. Their purchase keeps its value instead of trivialising the game they paid to enjoy.

Part Three

The Redesigned Game

Applying everything above. A new economy costed against a gathering model calibrated to real measurements, the gameplay systems that give the loop somewhere to go, and an endgame that continues past rebirth 30 instead of ending there.

What changes, in one view

Today

Fly out, fill inventory, fly back, dump into crusher, collect from furnace, sell. Repeat identically at rebirth 15 as at rebirth 3. Zones are interchangeable. Rebirth resets a counter.

Redesigned

NASO assigns you contracts. Rank gates which sectors you are cleared for. Each sector is denser, richer and more dangerous than the last. Rebirth is promotion — it opens the next sector and pays out permanent standing.

Today

Cost ×1.4 forever against a hard income ceiling. Rebirth 25 costs 10.6 hours, rebirth 30 costs 57 hours. The game has a mathematical end point nobody reaches.

Redesigned

Cost curve derived from a designed time curve — averaging ×1.28, decaying late. Thirty rebirths in 37 hours, no single rebirth over four hours, and an endgame built on optimisation rather than a steeper wall.

Today

Drones run only while away, cap at six hours, and hold output hostage until claimed. Autonomous Fleet supplies about 4% of what active play does.

Redesigned

Five drone tiers scaling to roughly a quarter of active income, running online once Drone Uplink is unlocked. Meaningful, never dominant — the answer to "play or idle" stays "play".

New gameplay systems

Core addition

NASO Contracts

The premise already says NASO sends workers on missions. Contracts make that literal. A board at the base offers three rotating jobs at a time, refreshing every twenty minutes or on completion.

Contract typeExampleReward
HaulDeliver 400 units of processed scrapCredits, scaled to rank
SalvageRecover 30 Meteor-class scrap specificallyCredits + scrap-type bonus
RushSell 5,000 credits' worth during a single market windowCredit multiplier for the next sale
SweepClear a marked debris cluster in the current sectorCharged scrap
CrewComplete a haul alongside another playerDouble rewards for both

Contracts cost almost nothing to build — they read existing counters. What they buy is enormous: a player always has a goal for the next fifteen minutes rather than an open-ended grind, and sessions gain a shape. They are also the natural home for daily and weekly objectives later.

Core addition

Company Rank

Rebirth stops being a counter and becomes a promotion. Rank is earned by rebirth count, which cannot be bought, and it gates sector access, contract tiers and gear.

R0–R3SweeperHome Belt only. Basic haul contracts. Learning the loop.
R4–R8SalvagerEmber Drift cleared. Salvage contracts unlock. First charged scrap.
R9–R14ReclaimerRusted Expanse cleared. Crew contracts unlock. Drone bay licensed.
R15–R21ForemanCinder Reach cleared. Can lead crew contracts and set shared objectives.
R22–R29OverseerThe Scarline cleared. Hazard contracts. Full drone fleet authority.
R30+Salvage MarshalSeasonal content, mastery tracks, leaderboards, cosmetic prestige.

Ranks give the game something to say at every rebirth. A promotion screen with a new title, a new sector clearance and a new contract type is dramatically more motivating than a number moving from 14 to 15.

Sectors

Zones with a reason to travel

Five sectors, each gated by rank. Density multiplies how fast you gather; yield multiplies how much crushed material each scrap produces; charged fraction multiplies value.

Endgame

Past rebirth 30 — mastery, not a steeper wall

The current curve simply keeps multiplying, which means the endgame is the same activity for longer. Rebirth 30 should instead open a different mode of play.

  • Seasonal sectors. A rotating sixth sector with its own modifiers, running six to eight weeks. New density, new hazards, a seasonal scrap type. This is the content source that scales — one sector per season keeps the game alive indefinitely without touching the core curve.
  • Mastery tracks per sector. Long-horizon objectives — clear ten thousand units from The Scarline, complete fifty hazard contracts — paying permanent small bonuses and cosmetics. Gives dedicated players a reason to keep playing one sector deeply.
  • Weekly leaderboards on credits earned, contracts completed and rare scrap recovered, resetting each week so newer players can compete. Competition is content the developers do not have to build twice.
  • Prestige cosmetics by rank. Jetpack trails, magnet skins, base decorations, name tags. Visible progress is its own retention loop and costs nothing to balance.
  • Endless rebirth with diminishing rewards past R30 — still climbing, but no longer the primary activity. The player who wants a number to chase keeps one; everyone else has contracts, seasons and mastery.
  • Crew guilds. Shared objectives across a group with pooled progress. The single strongest retention mechanic available, because it makes other players the reason to come back.
Depth

Variance and skill — the two missing content sources

  • Rare scrap with real variance. Scrap Fortune already implies a luck stat. Make it visible: occasional high-value finds, a rare scrap type worth twenty times normal. Every run becomes a lottery ticket rather than a known quantity.
  • Event variety over event size. Both current events are +75% to value. Density surges, zero-gravity windows, meteor showers dropping charged scrap, market crashes that reward holding inventory — each changes how an hour is played rather than just multiplying it.
  • Hazard sectors as a mini-game. High density with real danger and cargo lost on death. Rewards jetpack thrust and skill instead of time spent, and gives confident players a faster route.
  • Market timing as a skill. The ±30% swing and the two event types already create real decisions. Surface them properly — show the swing history, signal which event type is running — and selling becomes something players get good at.
  • Loadout choice. Magnets currently differ only in strength. Give some wider range and weaker pull, others the reverse, and a genuinely different playstyle appears at no content cost.

The optimal path, rebirth by rebirth

Costed against a gathering model calibrated to real measurements — rate = storage ÷ (30 s travel + storage ÷ (1.45 × pull × density)), which reproduces the observed 4.83 units/sec exactly. Player behaviour is modelled realistically rather than optimally: new players sell near the middle of the market, learn event timing around rebirth 6–9, and the crew boost saturates from rebirth 9 as players add anyone in the server. Highlighted rows are deliberate dips where a major unlock lands and the next rebirth is faster than the last.

Phase 1 · R0–4 · Hook

33 minutes for five rebirths. The player sees prestige before boredom sets in and learns that rebirth is a reward. Nova Pulse at R3 more than doubles storage.

Phase 2 · R5–9 · Systems

1h 32m. Ember Drift brings the first charged scrap and the first drone arrives. Star spending begins with the vault unlocks.

Phase 3 · R10–14 · Compounding

3h 30m. Rusted Expanse, Golden Touch coming online, crew saturating. The stretch where the game should feel best.

Phase 4 · R15–21 · The Climb

9h 48m. Astra and Cinder Reach land together — the largest jump in the game. Rebirths stretch but each still brings something.

Phase 5 · R22–29 · Mastery

21h 43m. The Scarline and the Sphere Magnet are the last power spikes. From here it is market timing, event timing, drone uptime and contracts.

Why the curve is shaped this way

  • Five rebirths in the first half hour. A player who has never rebirthed does not know why they would want to. Make the first one nearly free.
  • The dips are load-bearing. At R3, R8, R14, R19 and R22 a major unlock lands and the next rebirth is faster than the last. A monotonic climb reads as punishment; a sawtooth reads as progress.
  • No reward is more than two rebirths from the wall it solves. If the pain starts at R9 and the fix arrives at R15, players quit at R11.
  • Every unlock is worth at least a doubling. Anything smaller is invisible against a compounding cost curve.
  • The cost curve is not a fixed exponent. It averages ×1.28 and decays toward ×1.11 by R30, because time targets grow while gathering plateaus. No single fixed multiplier can produce a humane thirty-rebirth curve — that is the root of the current problem.

Star Exchange spend order

Stars are earned 1 per rebirth for R1–4, 2 each for R5–9, 3 each from R10.

  • R1–2 · bank them. Nothing is worth one star, and saving buys the important unlock sooner.
  • R3 · Furnace Vault (3★). The highest-value purchase in the game — furnaces survive rebirth, and so do the ingots inside them.
  • R5 · Gear Locker (2★). Magnets and jetpacks stop resetting, which is what makes the buying order above safe.
  • R7 · Crusher Vault (3★). Completes machine protection. Rebirth becomes close to lossless.
  • R8–12 · Golden Touch 1–5 (2★ each). The first pure multiplier; compounds with everything.
  • R13 · Star Vault (2★). Keeps 25% of credits through every reset thereafter.
  • R14 · Drone Uplink (3★). Drones run while online — the point the fleet becomes real.
  • R15 · Piping System (3★). Auto-feeds furnaces exactly when volume gets heavy.
  • R16+ · Star Magnet, Deep Pockets, Scrap Fortune, then the speed upgrades.
Appendix

Assumptions & Information Gaps

Everything resting on inference rather than measurement. Where a number was needed and unavailable it was derived logically and is flagged as such — none of it is presented as verified.

Assumptions used

  • 30 seconds travel overhead per trip, fitted from the measured 90-second trip at 435 storage and ×5.0 pull. If travel is nearer 20 seconds the model shifts about 15%, but the shape holds.
  • One storage unit yields one ingot. Scrap types occupy different amounts of storage and yield at least as much as they take, so income figures are a floor rather than an estimate.
  • Gathering scales linearly with magnet pull. Range may contribute separately.
  • Zone density multiplies gathering directly, and yield multiplies crushed output. Both are proposed systems, untested because zones are currently undifferentiated.
  • Crew boost saturates at ×1.40 from R9. The real cap is unknown.
  • Market behaviour by stage — +15% early rising to +85% once event timing is learned — is modelled from observation, not logged data.
  • Drone output is additive with active gathering once Drone Uplink is owned.

Still needed

  • Scrap-to-ingot ratio per type, and storage cost per type. Affects every income figure here.
  • Base crush time per scrap. Crusher multipliers are relative until this exists.
  • Crusher plot size — whether it matches the furnace plot.
  • Plot expansion cost per cell.
  • Whether machines have any capacity ceiling. None observed.
  • Equipment Shop contents — the third base shop is uncatalogued.
  • The 435 inventory arithmetic. Astra 210 plus Deep Pockets 15 accounts for 225; another 210 is unexplained.
  • Star cost curve — whether levels cost progressively more.
  • Ingot Rush duration, and whether other events drift like Star Flare.
  • Crew boost cap, and whether it counts friends or any player present.
